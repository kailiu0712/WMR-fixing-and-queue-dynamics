# Literature mapping: Phase 3 (WMR liquidity provision)

**How much of each source we read.** This varies by paper:
- **L1:** read from the arXiv HTML. Sections 2, 4 and 5 were read in full; the later sections only as summaries.
- **L2–L5:** the publisher pages refused automated access (HTTP 403, or a redirect stub). We rely only on each paper's stated scope. We make no claim about their detailed results.
- **L3:** the Bank of Canada working-paper abstract was read.

None of our experiments replicates any of these papers.

| | What the paper establishes | What we borrow | How our setting differs | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** Albers, Cucuringu, Howison & Shestopaloff (2025), *The Market Maker's Dilemma* (arXiv 2502.18625) | Live experiment on Binance's BTC perpetual: minimum-size maker orders kept at the touch, cancelled only when the price moves away. Fill probability and post-fill return are **negatively related**: orders followed by an adverse 5 s move are much more likely to fill. Queue position matters most when the near-side queue is large. They name the bias from observing others' orders, which are "implicitly conditioned on the trader's decision not to cancel". | Fill vs post-fill-return framing. Markout at a short horizon (they use 1 s and 5 s). Separate fill and return prediction, combined into an order-selection rule. | We observe **historical participant orders**, so every label already contains the participants' own cancellation policy, which is the exact bias L1 avoids. Fills here are *any execution within 5 s before exit*. L1 has no notion of a fixing window. | **Adaptation to a different dataset and market.** It is not a test of L1's numbers. |
| **L2** Garriott, van Kervel & Zoican (2025), *Queuing and Inventories in Limit Order Markets*, JFM 75 | Not read. Stated scope: queue position and inventories jointly shape liquidity provision and its adverse selection. | We treat queue-ahead as a determinant of **both** fill probability and markout (A2 strata, B features), not just of execution speed. | No inventories or trader identities. No equilibrium model is estimated. | **New empirical extension motivated by the literature.** |
| **L3** Krohn, Mueller & Whelan (2024), *FX Fixings and Returns around the Clock*, JF 79(1) | Bank of Canada WP 2021-48 abstract: long, significant intraday drifts that **reverse strongly** at the three major benchmark fixes. The USD appreciates before the fixes and depreciates after. This is explained by order imbalances before the fixes, from large bank desks, and by dealer inventory management. | Motivation: price behaviour and maker markouts may differ around the fix. | One instrument (6E futures), 66 trading days, seconds-scale markouts. Our earlier first pass found **no** reversal in 6E after the WMR window (corr 0.04, p = 0.74), but that test is not comparable to theirs (horizon, market, sample). | **Not directly comparable.** Motivation only. |
| **L4** Muhle-Karbe, Oomen & Weber (2025), *Hedging of Fixing Exposure*, Math. Finance 35(4) | Not read. Stated scope: a model of how dealers time the execution of fixing hedges under transient and permanent impact. | Motivation for why fixing demand concentrates and shapes short-term liquidity inside the window. | We see no client exposures or hedging strategies. | **Conceptual motivation only.** |
| **L5** Huang, Lehalle & Rosenbaum (2015), *The Queue-Reactive Model*, JASA 110 | Not re-read here. Stated scope: order-event intensities depend on the current state of the order book, i.e. queue sizes. | Condition every comparison on the pre-entry book state (queue-ahead, depth, imbalance, spread, recent flow) before attributing a difference to WMR. | We do not estimate the queue-reactive model. | **Motivation for the conditioning design.** |

## Implementation notes from L6, checked against our data

- **The book changes only on A, C, M and R** (existing `main.Book`). F and T are counted, but never applied to the book.
- **A passive fill** is an F on the order's own id, at the order's own limit price.
  - The existing EDA label also counted F records that sit at a **different** price and carry a T on the same order id. Those are participants repricing a resting order across the spread, i.e. the case "aggressor already on the book gets a Fill record".
  - That is 2.5% of the old WMR "fills", 2.1% nearby and 3.2% normal. We relabel them as voluntary exits (`aggressive_reprice`, label 0).
  - After the correction, 100% of fills are at the entry price. Side is the order's side, or `N` on 1.1% of fills.
- **Packet boundaries:** pre-entry features use completed packets only (`is_last` = F_LAST). The entry packet itself is excluded, except for the add row's own size and queue-ahead.
